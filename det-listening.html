Strategy: Listen for WHO / WHAT / WHERE First

WHO

Identify the speaker's role and the listener's role. Student? Professor? Librarian? This determines register and expected vocabulary.

WHAT

What is the purpose? Requesting an extension? Asking for clarification? Complaining about a grade? The goal shapes every response.

WHERE

Campus office? Library? Dormitory? Classroom? Location provides context clues for the vocabulary and tone you will encounter.

Connected Speech Training

Native speakers do not pronounce each word in isolation. Words connect, merge, and reduce. Understanding connected speech is the difference between hearing individual words and hearing meaning.

Toggle All Reduced Forms
Linking (连读)
Turn off the light.
Tur-noff the light.
/tɜːrˈnɔːf/ — consonant + vowel linking
I need an answer.
I nee-da nanswer.
/niːdəˈnænsər/ — consonant + vowel
Pick it up.
Pi-ki-tup.
/ˈpɪkɪˈtʌp/ — consonant + vowel chain
Reduction (弱读)
What do you want?
Whaddaya want?
/ˈwʌdəjə wɑːnt/ — "do you" reduces
I am going to study.
I'm gonna study.
/ˈgʌnə/ — "going to" reduces
Do you want to go?
Ya wanna go?
/jəˈwʌnə goʊ/ — multiple reductions
Elision (省音)
Next week
Nex week
/neks wiːk/ — /t/ dropped between consonants
I must go.
I mus' go.
/mʌs goʊ/ — /t/ elided before consonant
The first thing
The firs' thing
/fɜːrs θɪŋ/ — /t/ elided, /s/ remains
Assimilation (同化)
Did you see it?
Di-joo see it?
/ˈdɪdʒuː/ — /d/ + /j/ becomes /dʒ/
Would you help?
Woul-joo help?
/ˈwʊdʒuː/ — /d/ + /j/ becomes /dʒ/
In practice
Im practice
/ɪm ˈpræktɪs/ — /n/ assimilates to /m/ before /p/
💡
DET Connected Speech Tip: DET audio uses Standard American English at natural speed. Pay special attention to "did you" → "didja", "want to" → "wanna", and the disappearing /t/ between consonants. These are the most common sound changes that cause DET test-takers to miss words they actually know.

Video Shadowing

Shadowing means repeating audio simultaneously as you hear it, like a simultaneous echo. It trains your brain to process sound at native speed without the mental translation step.

1

Watch

Watch the video once with subtitles. Understand the content fully. Note any unfamiliar vocabulary.

2

Listen

Watch again without reading subtitles. Focus purely on the sound. Notice rhythm, stress, and intonation patterns.

3

Shadow

Play the video. Repeat the audio simultaneously, matching pace and intonation. Keep going even if you stumble.

4

Record

Record your shadowing and compare with the original. Identify gaps between your production and the native speaker's.

Shadowing Protocol: 10 minutes daily. One video. Cycle through the four steps. After one week on the same video, you should be able to shadow it with near-perfect synchronization. This means your brain is processing English at native speed.

Gap-Fill Dictation

Listen to a sentence with one word or phrase removed. Fill the gap. Progress from single words to phrases to reduced-form recognition.

Daily Practice Protocol

A 20-minute daily routine combining all skills. Consistency beats cramming.

Time Activity Focus Goal
0-3 min Connected Speech Warm-up 5 reduced-form phrases, spoken aloud Prime your ear for sound changes
3-8 min Gap-Fill Dictation 5 sentences, progressive difficulty Train precise word recognition
8-15 min Video Shadowing One short video, 4-step cycle Build processing speed and prosody
15-20 min Scenario Practice 1 Interactive Listening scenario Apply WHO/WHAT/WHERE framework

Self-Assessment Checklist

  • I can identify linking in natural speech
  • I recognize reduced forms (gonna, wanna, whaddaya)
  • I can shadow a 60-second clip with 80% accuracy
  • I can extract WHO/WHAT/WHERE from audio scenarios
  • I score 80%+ on gap-fill dictation
  • I practice listening for 20 minutes daily
