# PART 1: DISCOURSE MARKERS BY FUNCTION

## 1. FILLER WORDS / STALLING PHRASES
**Function:** Buy time while thinking. Soften speech. Sound natural, not robotic.

| # | Phrase | Example Sentence |
|---|--------|-----------------|
| 1 | I mean… | "I mean, it's not something I think about every day." |
| 2 | like | "It was, like, the most incredible experience I've ever had." |
| 3 | well | "Well, I suppose it depends on the situation." |
| 4 | you know | "It's one of those things that, you know, just happens naturally." |
| 5 | maybe / perhaps | "Maybe it's because people are more health-conscious these days." |
| 6 | let's say | "Let's say you're in a foreign country and don't speak the language." |
| 7 | honestly / frankly speaking | "Honestly, I'd never really thought about it until now." |
| 8 | to tell you the truth | "To tell you the truth, I'm not the biggest fan of crowded places." |
| 9 | truth be told | "Truth be told, I find it a bit overwhelming sometimes." |
| 10 | basically / actually | "Basically, it comes down to personal preference." |

| 12 | I've never thought about this before | "I've never thought about this question, to be honest." |
| 13 | Let me think about that… | "Let me think about that… I'd say it varies from person to person." |

## 4. DIFFICULT QUESTION STRATEGIES
**Function:** Handle questions you can't answer. Critical for Band 7+ fluency.

### 4a. Ask for Clarification
| Phrase |
|--------|
| Sorry, would you mind repeating the question? |
| Sorry sir/ma'am, I didn't really get your question. Could you please explain it? |
| Sorry, I didn't quite catch your question. Could you repeat it? |

### 4b. Part 1 Method (Short Answer)
- "Well to be honest, gotta say this is a tricky question for me."
- "Off the top of my head, I really dunno where to start."
- "But if I had to guess, I'd say…"
- "But if you hold my feet to the fire, I'd say…"

### 4c. Part 2 Method (Long Turn)
- "Well, to be honest, as soon as I saw this topic, the first thing that popped into my mind was actually… because off the top of my head, I could not think of any… so I'll go with that if you don't mind."

### 4d. Part 3 Method (Discussion)
- "That's a great question! But I have to admit that I'm not really an expert on this topic. But based on what I've noticed though, I'd say…"
- "Maybe I can give you my two cents based on my observation."

### 4e. Handling Pauses / Mental Blocks
- I've hit a mental block.
- Well actually, I've just lost my train of thought, lemme rephrase.
- Well my mind just went completely blank here.
- I'm drawing a blank here.
- Sorry that I couldn't provide you with anything useful.

# PART 2: LOGIC CHAINS / TRAINS OF THOUGHT

## Chain 1: Answer-Detail (AD)
**Use for:** Part 1 and Part 3 short answers
**Structure:** Give answer → Explain why → Give example (optional)

**Checklist:**
1. Use logical connectors — avoid "stacking" information
2. Identify why speech flow broke during playback
3. Use "rephrase" structures when stuck
4. Use natural expressions in the Answer part
5. Paraphrase Yes/No in the Answer
6. Paraphrase the verb from the question (e.g., "like" → "I'm quite into")
7. Use adverbials for intensity ("I'm quite/really/absolutely into…")
8. Use multiple ways to express "because" in the Detail part
9. Use multiple ways to express "another reason is that"
10. Give concrete examples for familiar topics
11. Avoid over-specific examples for unfamiliar topics

**Discourse markers for AD:**
- Answer: Definitely / I'd say so / It depends / Well, to be honest…
- Detail: Because / The reason is that / I guess it's because…
- Example: For instance / A personal example is…

## Chain 2: Y/N Question → Answer-Detail-Example
**Use for:** Yes/No questions in Part 1 and Part 3

**Y分支:**
- Definitely! / Absolutely! / 100% / Totally!
- I'd say so. / I'm inclined to say yes. / Pretty much so.
- It appears so. / Presumably yes. / Signs point to yes.

**N分支:**
- Definitely not! / Absolutely no! / Zero chance.
- I wouldn't say so. / I'm afraid not. / I'm afraid that's not the case.
- Not likely. / I beg to differ. / Probably not. / That's a long shot.

**It Depends分支:**
- It depends. / It could go either way.
- Sometimes… However, in most cases…

**Detail:** 5W1H — Why / How you feel / How often
**3rd Point:** On top of that + Detail OR A great example of this is…

## Chain 3: 比较类 (Comparison)
**Use for:** "Which do you prefer?" / "A or B?" questions

**Structure:**
1. State preference: "I'd go with… / … would be my preference"
2. Compare both sides: "not as … as" / "much/far/way + comparative"
3. Justify with degree adverbials

**Discourse markers:**
- Compared to A, B is much more…
- A is not as … as B
- While A is …, B tends to be…
- I'd probably go with… because…

## Chain 4: 总分-分情况 (General → By Situation)
**Use for:** "Do people in your country…?" / "Is it common to…?"

**Structure:**
1. 总述 (General statement): "It really depends on the situation."
2. 分情况1: "In terms of [situation A], …"
3. 分情况2: "When it comes to [situation B], …"
4. 总结: "So basically, it varies from case to case."

**Discourse markers:**
- Topic introducers: In terms of / When it comes to / As for / Regarding
- Cause: The reason for this difference is that…
- Sequencing: To begin with… / Other than that…
- Rephrase: What I mean is that… (when stuck)
- Summary: At the end of the day / So basically

## Chain 5: 总分-分群体 (General → By Group)
**Use for:** "Do different age groups…?" / "How does this vary among people?"

**Structure:**
1. 总述: "The impact varies significantly among different demographics."
2. 分群体1 (Elderly): "In terms of elderly people, they tend to be more traditional…"
3. 分群体2 (Young): "When it comes to younger generations, they are generally more…"
4. 对比 / 总结

**Demographic dimensions for slicing:**
- Age groups (elderly / middle-aged / young / teenagers)
- Socioeconomic (privileged / underprivileged)
- Urban vs rural
- Jobs / Responsibilities (civil servants / parents / students)
- Interests / Habits (outdoor types / homebodies / creatives)

**Discourse markers:**
- Topic introducers: In terms of / From …'s standpoint / When it comes to
- Contrast: However / In contrast / Whereas / While
- Examples: Take … as an example / For instance
- Emphasis: What stands out for this group is…

## Chain 6: 总分-罗列 (General → List)
**Use for:** "What kinds of…?" / "What are the benefits of…?"

**Structure:**
1. 总述: Comment on WHY the question matters
2. 罗列1: "The first thing I'd mention is…"
3. 罗列2: "On top of that…"
4. 罗列3: "And last but not least…"

**Quantity alternatives for "plenty of":**
tons of / loads of / a plethora of / a myriad of / a great variety of / a wide range of

**Discourse markers:**
- Sequencing: First / On top of that / Last but not least
- Examples: A classic example is / Take … for example
- Emphasis: The most prominent [example/reason] is…

## Chain 7: 总分-强调 (General → Emphasize One)
**Use for:** "What's the most important…?"

**Structure:**
1. 总述: "There are several [reasons/factors], but if I had to pick the most important one…"
2. 强调: "What really matters here is…"
3. 论证: "This is largely due to the fact that…"
4. 举例: "A striking example of this is…"

**Discourse markers:**
- Emphasis: What … is / The thing is / It all boils down to
- Cause: The most prominent reason is that
- Flagging: My point here is / If I had to choose just one factor

## Chain 8: General-Specific-Opposite (Dialectical)
**Use for:** Part 3 abstract discussion questions

**Structure:**
1. General observation: "Generally speaking, most people would say…"
2. Specific angle: "However, if we look at this more specifically…"
3. Opposite / Counterpoint: "On the flip side, there's also the argument that…"
4. Synthesis: "So at the end of the day, I'd say it's not black and white."

## Chain 9: 总分-举例 (General → Example)
**Structure:** State view → Give striking example → Explain significance

## Chain 10: Wh-Question → Direct Answer + Detail
**Use for:** What/Why/How/When/Where questions

**Structure:**
1. Direct answer
2. Explain why
3. Example or contrast
